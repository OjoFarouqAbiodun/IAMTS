## 1. Introduction

The Students' Industrial Work Experience Scheme (SIWES) is a six-month,
work-based training programme that is compulsory for all science and
technology students in Nigerian universities. Its purpose is to expose
students to real industrial practice so that the theoretical knowledge
gained in the classroom can be connected to actual work situations. SIWES
is coordinated by the university but takes place inside an approved
organization, where the student works under the supervision of an
experienced professional.

I was placed at the ICT department of the Lagos State Ministry of
Innovation, Science and Technology, located at the Alausa Secretariat in
Ikeja, Lagos. The attachment lasted six months, during which I worked
alongside the department's technical and administrative staff, observed
how government ICT operations are carried out, and carried out assigned
duties that gave me hands-on experience in information technology
management, database handling, and software development.

This report covers my experience during the attachment — the organization
I worked in, the duties I performed, the skills I acquired, the
challenges I faced, and the recommendations I have based on what I
observed.

## 2. The Organization

### 2.1 About the Ministry

The Lagos State Ministry of Innovation, Science and Technology is one of
the government ministries under the Lagos State Government. It is
headquartered at the Alausa Secretariat in Ikeja, and its mandate is to
drive innovation, promote science and technology development, and oversee
technology-related policies and projects across the state.

The ministry is responsible for coordinating initiatives that improve how
technology is used in governance, education, health, and other sectors of
the state. It also supports local innovation hubs, technology startups,
and research activities that contribute to the economic development of
Lagos State.

### 2.2 The ICT Department

Within the ministry, the ICT department is responsible for managing the
information technology infrastructure that the ministry depends on. This
includes maintaining the computers, printers, servers, networking
equipment, and software that staff use every day. The department also
handles internal technical support, system upgrades, and coordination
with external vendors when new equipment needs to be procured.

The ICT department is relatively small, with a handful of technicians and
support staff who handle everything from hardware repairs to network
troubleshooting and asset record keeping. It was within this department
that I carried out my SIWES attachment.

### 2.3 Physical Setting

The ministry is located within the Alausa Government Secretariat, which
houses several ministries and agencies. The ICT department occupies a
section of the secretariat building, with workstations, a small server
room, a maintenance workshop area, and a store where spare parts and
equipment are kept. The environment was professional and structured, with
clear lines of reporting and defined responsibilities for each staff
member.

## 3. Duties Performed

During my time at the ICT department, I was involved in a range of
activities that gave me exposure to both the technical and administrative
sides of ICT management in a government setting. My duties included:

### 3.1 Asset Registration and Record Keeping

One of my earliest assignments was helping the department organize its
asset records. At the time, the records were kept in paper registers and
Excel spreadsheets, with different staff members maintaining their own
copies. I helped consolidate these records, verify asset tags and serial
numbers, and update the master spreadsheet with current information about
each item's location, condition, and the staff member it was assigned to.

This work gave me a clear picture of the problems with manual record
keeping — the difficulty of searching for information, the risk of
duplication, and the lack of a single source of truth. It was this
observation that eventually inspired my project, IAMTS.

### 3.2 Hardware Maintenance Assistance

I assisted the technicians with basic hardware maintenance tasks,
including:

- Diagnosing and fixing desktop computers that would not boot or had
  hardware faults.
- Replacing faulty components such as hard drives, RAM modules, power
  supply units, and toner cartridges in printers.
- Cleaning and maintaining equipment to keep them in good working
  condition.
- Setting up new workstations for staff members who were newly posted to
  the ministry.

### 3.3 Software Installation and Configuration

I participated in the installation and configuration of software on
departmental computers. This included installing operating systems
(Windows 10 and Windows 11), productivity software (Microsoft Office),
antivirus programs, and specialized applications as required by different
units. I also helped configure printers on the network and troubleshoot
software-related issues reported by staff.

### 3.4 Network and Connectivity Support

Although the department had a dedicated network team, I was allowed to
observe and assist with basic networking tasks. This included checking
network cable connections, helping to configure IP addresses, testing
connectivity between workstations and the server, and documenting the
network layout of the department.

### 3.5 Email and Communication Management

I helped manage the department's email accounts using Microsoft Outlook.
This included setting up email profiles for new staff, organizing mail
folders, and ensuring that important correspondence was properly filed.
I also used Outlook to schedule meetings and send calendar invites on
behalf of my supervisor.

### 3.6 Documentation and Reporting

A significant part of my duties involved documentation. I prepared
technical reports, memos, and inter-departmental letters using Microsoft
Word. I also maintained Excel spreadsheets for tracking maintenance
schedules, asset movements, and equipment service histories. At various
points during the attachment, I was asked to compile data from different
sources and present it in a format that management could review.

### 3.7 Project Development

Towards the middle and end of my attachment, with the permission and
guidance of my industrial supervisor, I began developing the ICT Assets
Maintenance and Tracking System (IAMTS). This involved analysing the
existing manual processes, designing a database to replace the
spreadsheets, building the web application, and testing it before
demonstrating it to the department. The development of this project
allowed me to apply the software development and database design knowledge
I had learned in school to a real-world problem I had directly observed.

## 4. Skills Acquired

The SIWES attachment gave me a wide range of skills that I could not have
gained from classroom lectures alone. These skills fall into two broad
categories: technical skills and soft skills.

### 4.1 Technical Skills

1. **Hardware troubleshooting and repair** — I learned how to diagnose
   common PC faults, replace components, and perform preventive
   maintenance on desktops, laptops, and printers.

2. **Operating system installation and configuration** — I became
   proficient in installing Windows operating systems from scratch,
   including driver installation, updates, and user account setup.

3. **Database design and management** — Through my project, I learned how
   to design a relational database from scratch, normalize it to Third
   Normal Form, write SQL queries, and connect it to a web application.
   This was directly applied in the IAMTS project using MySQL.

4. **Web application development** — I gained practical experience in
   full-stack web development using Node.js, Express, and vanilla HTML,
   CSS, and JavaScript. I learned how to build RESTful APIs, implement
   authentication and authorization, and deploy a working application.

5. **Network basics** — I learned about IP addressing, cable management,
   network troubleshooting, and how a small government office network is
   structured.

6. **Microsoft Office proficiency** — I improved my skills in Word (mail
   merge, document formatting, CV creation), Excel (formulas, charts,
   data analysis), PowerPoint (presentations with animations), Access
   (basic tables, queries, forms), and Outlook (email and calendar
   management).

7. **Security awareness** — Working on IAMTS taught me about application
   security — password hashing, session management, CSRF protection, XSS
   prevention, and the importance of audit logging in a multi-user system.

### 4.2 Soft Skills

1. **Professional communication** — I learned how to write formal memos,
   inter-departmental letters, and technical reports for a government
   setting. I also improved my ability to communicate technical ideas to
   non-technical people.

2. **Time management** — Balancing my daily duties with the development of
   my project taught me how to plan my time, set priorities, and meet
   deadlines.

3. **Teamwork and collaboration** — I worked closely with the ICT
   technicians and administrative staff, learning how to contribute to a
   team, take instructions, and share knowledge.

4. **Problem-solving** — Whether it was a computer that would not boot, a
   printer that kept jamming, or a database design challenge in my project,
   I learned to approach problems systematically and find practical
   solutions.

5. **Initiative and self-direction** — My supervisor gave me the
   independence to explore problems and propose solutions on my own. This
   built my confidence and taught me to take ownership of my work.

6. **Adaptability** — Working in a government ministry required me to
   adapt to formal processes, organizational hierarchy, and the pace of
   government operations, which was quite different from the academic
   environment.

## 5. Challenges Encountered

### 5.1 Incomplete and Scattered Asset Records

When I arrived, the department's asset records were spread across
different spreadsheets maintained by different people, with no single
centralized source. Some assets had no tags, some had outdated
information, and some were not recorded at all. Consolidating these
records was time-consuming and required going physically from office to
office to verify what was where. This challenge was what motivated the
IAMTS project.

### 5.2 Resistance to Change

When I proposed building a web application to replace the manual process,
there was some initial hesitation from staff who were comfortable with
the existing spreadsheet system. Some were not very familiar with web
applications and were concerned about learning a new system. I addressed
this by making the interface simple and demonstrating the advantages of
the new system over the old process, such as the ability to search
instantly and generate reports with one click.

### 5.3 Limited Internet and Power Supply

The ministry experienced occasional power outages, and the internet
connection was not always stable. This affected work that depended on
downloading resources or accessing online documentation. I learned to
plan my work around these limitations — doing offline tasks during outages
and using local copies of documentation where possible.

### 5.4 Balancing SIWES Duties with Project Development

Since the IAMTS project was developed during the attachment, I had to
balance my regular departmental duties with the time needed for software
development. There were days when maintenance requests or urgent tasks
took priority, which slowed down the project. I learned to manage my
time better and work on the project during quieter periods.

### 5.5 Hardware Compatibility Issues

During hardware maintenance, I sometimes encountered older machines with
proprietary components or limited driver support, making repairs more
difficult. I learned to research compatibility options and work with
available parts rather than waiting for specific replacements.

## 7. Recommendations

### 7.1 To the Ministry

1. **Adopt a digital asset management system.** The manual paper and
   spreadsheet system is slow, error-prone, and difficult to report on.
   A system like IAMTS, or a similar tool, would significantly improve
   how the department tracks its ICT assets and handles maintenance
   requests.

2. **Centralize the ICT asset database.** Even if a full web application
   is not immediately feasible, the department should at least maintain a
   single, shared database (even a well-structured Access database or a
   shared Excel file on a network drive) that all staff update from the
   same location.

3. **Provide regular training for ICT staff.** Technology changes quickly,
   and the staff would benefit from periodic training on new tools,
   security best practices, and maintenance techniques.

4. **Improve internet and power infrastructure.** Reliable power and
   internet would improve productivity and make it easier to adopt digital
   tools.

### 7.2 To the University

1. **Start SIWES placement preparation earlier.** Some students were
   unsure about what to expect. An earlier orientation session with
   guidelines on what to observe and document would help students make
   better use of their time.

2. **Encourage students to identify project topics during SIWES.** Many
   students wait until after the attachment to start thinking about their
   projects. If students were encouraged to observe problems from day one,
   they would have more meaningful projects to present.

3. **Maintain a database of approved SIWES organizations.** Having a
   curated and verified list of organizations with ICT departments,
   database teams, and development opportunities would help students find
   placements that are relevant to their course of study.

### 7.3 To Future SIWES Students

1. **Be observant.** The best project ideas come from noticing problems in
   the workplace. Pay attention to manual processes that could be
   automated.

2. **Be proactive.** Do not wait to be told everything. Ask questions,
   offer to help, and volunteer for tasks that will give you new
   experiences.

3. **Document everything.** Keep a log of your daily activities, the
   skills you are learning, and the challenges you face. This will make
   writing your SIWES report much easier at the end.

4. **Start your project early.** Do not wait until the last month of your
   attachment to begin development. The more time you have, the better
   your project will be.
